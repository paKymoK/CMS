When delegations from Hanoi and Beijing sat down this June, the agenda was not whether the two economies would cooperate on AI — that question was settled years ago. It was how to do it in a way that holds up under real production workloads.

Why this dialogue, and why now

Cross-border technology trade between the two countries has grown steadily, but most of it still runs through hardware and manufacturing. Software and AI services are the next layer — and they need shared expectations around data handling, model governance and talent mobility before they can scale.

The dialogue brought together ministry representatives, enterprise CIOs and delivery partners like CMC Global to work through those expectations in concrete terms.

Three themes that came up in every session

  • 01Interoperable AI standards. Aligning on evaluation and documentation practices so models built in one market can be audited in the other.
  • 02Data residency by design. Architectures that keep regulated data in-country while still sharing model improvements.
  • 03Joint talent pipelines. Exchange programmes and shared certification tracks for ML engineers.

What it means for enterprises

For companies already running AI pilots, the practical takeaway is that governance work done now will pay off twice: once for domestic compliance, and again when the same systems are extended across the border.

"The teams that win the next five years are the ones treating governance as an engineering problem, not a paperwork one."

— Minh Tran, CMC Global

Where to start

Inventory the models you already run in production, document their training data lineage, and pick one workflow where a cross-border team could contribute. That is enough to be ready when the frameworks discussed in Hanoi become formal guidance.

What comes next

Working groups will report back at the next session later this year. CMC Global will continue contributing on delivery practices and talent programmes, and we will share updates here as they land.
